## Description

```
You start with a teenager nobody has heard of, in a division you choose, in a year you choose. Forty
fights later he is either a champion, a gatekeeper, or retired at thirty with a record that reads like a
warning. The Final Bell is a boxing career simulator about the long haul: the club shows, the step-ups,
the night the plan goes wrong, and the belt at the end of it if you get there.

FIGHTS ARE FOUGHT, NOT ROLLED

Nothing is decided by a single dice throw. Every bout runs round by round, punch by punch, and it is
called as it happens — who is landing, who is fading, when a cut opens, when a man's legs go. You watch
it at your own pace: half speed, normal, double, or paused, and you can scroll back through the whole
fight afterwards. If it goes the distance, all three judges read their cards, one at a time.

A WORLD THAT DOES NOT WAIT FOR YOU

Between your fights the sport keeps running. Contenders win and lose, champions are made and stripped,
prospects come through and old fighters retire. Turn down a fight and the man you avoided may take the
title while you sit out. Rankings, belts and reputations all move without your permission, and you can
stop and watch any bout in your own division as it happens.

A CAREER WITH A SHAPE

Six-rounders first. Ten rounds once you have a record; twelve once you are somebody. Regional belts
before world titles, eliminators before shots. Fights are made at real venues, on cards that fit what
you are — a small hall on a club show early, a championship bill at Caesars Palace if you get that far —
and you see the card, the undercard and the tale of the tape before you walk out.

THE RECORD BOOKS

Every year the sport hands out Fighter of the Year, Fight of the Year, Knockout of the Year and Upset of
the Year, and every citation can be opened and read. Retired greats go into a Hall of Fame ranked on what
they actually did. Your own career is tracked in full: rounds boxed, punches landed, title reigns, days
as champion, longest winning streak.

OR RUN THE WHOLE SPORT WITH NOBODY IN IT

Universe mode drops the player entirely and simulates the sport for as long as you like, so you can watch
a whole era of a division produce its own champions, rivalries and Hall of Famers.

WHAT IT ISN'T

No accounts, no sign-in, no adverts, no in-app purchases, no network connection of any kind. It runs
offline, saves to one file on your own machine, and collects nothing about you.
```

## One thing to decide before you submit

The roster ships with the names of real boxers — Hagler, Holmes, Minter, Duran and hundreds more. Nothing
about that is settled law in your favour: a person's name and likeness carry publicity rights in most US
states, and Store certification has an intellectual-property step that can reject or later pull an app
over exactly this. Plenty of sims do the same thing and are never troubled; some get a takedown years
later.

The options, in the order they cost you:

1. **Ship as is.** Most likely nothing happens. If something does, it is a takedown notice, not a
   lawsuit, and you would then have to do option 2 in a hurry.
2. **Ship with generated names only** and keep the real roster as an optional file users can drop in
   themselves. The generator already produces 94,050 era-appropriate combinations across 12 cultures, so
   the game works exactly as well without a single real name in it.
3. **Rename the roster to near-misses.** Cheap, and fools nobody, including a lawyer.

This is your call, not a certification blocker — but it is worth making deliberately rather than
discovering it on a rejection notice.
